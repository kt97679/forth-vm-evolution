# Thumb-2 for the ARM engines, and LLVM's machine outliner (Iteration 742)

The owner (741, "can you think of anything else we can check?"; 742, "continue"): embedded toolchains' size practice
- the two flags such guides name first, tried on the roles.

## The ARM engines are A32 - all of them

No ARM build names a mode. clang's cross build (evolve.py's ARM_CROSS_CC: --target=arm-linux-gnueabihf -march=armv7-a)
defines no __thumb__: A32. The Tegra's GCC (Gentoo 16.2.1): its three GCC designs' engines at 726 (results/rage-designs-
726.md) were 16,782, 13,014 and 11,158 B - this sandbox's builds of the same designs with Ubuntu's cross GCC 13 are
16,862, 13,070 and 11,186 B with -marm and 12,506, 9,754 and 8,410 B in Thumb-2 (that GCC's default, --with-mode=thumb).
So the Tegra builds A32 too, and every ARM design - clang's and GCC's - was measured in one instruction set: the roles'
ARMv7 comparisons are not confounded. (`cc -dM -E - </dev/null | grep -c __thumb2__` on the Tegra would say 0.)

Every build passed the lab's alive check (the corpus, the kernel, far, full) and gave the reference's fib and apps
(tools/arm-cross-check.py). The records' size is the x86-64 build's, so none of this moves a record or a role; it is what
ships for ARM (relf's `make arm`). The speed on a Cortex-A9 is the question: Thumb-2's narrower instructions fit the
cache better and decode as fast on most cores, but some sequences take more instructions - only the Tegra can say.

As a gene, queued for the series' end (results/queued-thumb/ORDER.md): `thumb` adds -mthumb to an ARM build alone. Its
checks - ids of the 6,330 recorded designs the same with and without it; mutate() (4,000 draws) and random_genome()
(2,000) never set it (HELD); the six variants' x86 builds at 8- and 4-byte cells byte for byte their parents'; the
clang variants' ARM builds the table's Thumb-2 column (arm-cross-check: alive, identical); the Tegra line's sample
(xcheck.py --sample --cell 4 --bundle --arm --also) bundling the three Thumb-2 engines and leaving the GCC three to the
host; export-designs.py and export-arm.py on two variants - each Makefile's `forth-arm` carries -mthumb and builds the
lab's bytes (8,848 B for portable-knee's), running. With Ubuntu's cross GCC (Thumb-2 by default) a GCC variant is its
parent's bytes; on the Tegra (A32 by default) it is the Thumb-2 build. The follow-up after the series measures the
pairs on the Tegra (results/candidates-thumb-742.md on its list; results/drafts/thumb-pairs-742.py, self-test PASS).
