# The JIT roles' stack gap priced (Iteration 739; the finding: results/drafts/data-layout2-738.txt)

The owner (739): "continue" - the fixes priced. On the JIT roles without the guard gene (small-jit, fastest-x86-32,
fastest-x86-64) two runaways in JIT code die 139 with nothing on stderr and the output lost: a data-stack push (the
JIT's code has no dsp bound; the push runs down through the engine's one RWE segment, the fault handler included) and,
found now, native recursion (`: R3 RECURSE 1 ;` - JIT'd calls nest on the C stack; at its end the fault handler has no
stack to run on: the kernel's SI_KERNEL SIGSEGV). Both at both widths (fastest-x86-32's i386 build has its JIT).
best-all-round (JIT and guard) names both.

Three variants, each the role's lab build (results/drafts/jit-guard-739.py MASTER SLIM; medians of 7, cachegrind):
  base   the role as designs/ has it;
  guard  the same genome with guard=1 - the gene: two guard pages, the handler on its own stack, the interpreter's
         pushes unchecked (vm-lab.c's PUSH and RPUSH without the compare);
  slim   results/drafts/jit-guard-slim-739.patch on master: for X_JIT designs without the gene one unreadable page at
         the data stack's floor (the interpreter's bound a page above it, so it still names the overflow first) and
         the handler on a 16 KB stack of its own (sigaltstack, SA_ONSTACK in every fault installer - none set: the
         current stack, so no other design behaves differently).

## Where guard's instructions go

The unchecked pushes are the interpreter's, so the gain is where the interpreter runs - the compile-heavy workloads -
not in JIT code. guard / base, cachegrind, 8-byte cells:

| role | corpus | kernel | apps | fib | loop |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| fastest-x86-64 | 0.9244 | 0.9431 | 0.9937 | 0.9999 | 0.9998 |
| small-jit | 0.9164 | 0.9316 | 0.9368 | 0.9999 | 0.9998 |

CPU time in this sandbox (fastest-x86-64, 15 alternations a workload, median of the pairs' ratios; noisy - runs of
3-17 ms on a shared machine): kernel 0.952, corpus 0.952, fib 0.993, loop 1.022, bubble 0.998, ackermann 1.050, apps
1.006; geometric mean 0.996. The direction the instructions give, on the two workloads they move; the rest within the
noise. The laptop's alternated measurement is what decides a speed role.

## What it comes to

- **The guard gene on the three JIT roles** closes both gaps, names each trap, and runs the interpreted part 6-8 %
  fewer instructions (small-jit's apps 6 % - it translates fewer words): +144-272 B at 8-byte cells, +152-408 at 4,
  memory +4-8 KB at 8-byte cells (fastest-x86-32 268 against 260: the 8 KB rule's edge) and -4 to +4 at 4. As designs
  they are new - 7a6c182638 (small-jit's), b7a09aec76 (fastest-x86-32's), fba7d5be3d (fastest-x86-64's), never measured
  by the search. They are challengers for the roles' follow-up chains after the series: the rule (1 % smaller, no class
  2 % slower, memory within 8 KB; a speed role's challenger 2 % faster at 2 SE) decides, on the laptop's time.
- **slim** (+112-128 B at 8-byte cells, +176-208 at 4; no instruction, no page) is the fallback if guard loses on time:
  the corruption and the lost output gone, the message generic. A draft patch, not queued - it changes JIT designs'
  engines, so it would wait for the series' end like step 2.
- **A dsp check on the JIT's loop back-edges** - not built: it would cost time in JIT code, where these roles win, to
  give what guard gives with none; and native recursion would still need the handler's own stack.
